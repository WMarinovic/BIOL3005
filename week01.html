What the task is doing

While you fixate the cross, anything to its left reaches your right hemisphere first, and anything to its right reaches your left hemisphere first.

On a same side match, both copies of the number land in one hemisphere. On an across match, they land in different hemispheres, so the comparison needs the corpus callosum.

The display lasts 195 ms, which is about the time it takes to launch an eye movement. Switch the display to 2 seconds and notice how your eyes jump to the numbers: once that happens, both hemispheres see everything and the logic of the task is lost.

Run the task

The task runs full screen and takes about 12 minutes. You need a keyboard; sound is optional (a short beep warns you that the numbers are about to appear).

  1. Sit centred in front of the screen, at a comfortable arm's length.
  2. Keep your eyes on the cross in the middle of the screen for the whole task.
  3. Press the spacebar only when the bottom number matches one of the top two. Otherwise, do nothing.

There are three blocks (digits, dots and mixed), each with a short practice. When you finish, your results appear on the My results tab, ready to share.

Settings for tutors

Each block has 8 practice trials with feedback, then the test trials: a quarter same-side matches, a quarter across matches and half without a match, with the bottom number equally often on the left and the right. The display lasts 195 ms, the response window 2100 ms, and a fixation cross precedes each display by 2100 ms (beep at 1050 ms), as in the Inquisit script. Block order is assigned at random from three Latin-square orders, and the mixed version (digits or dots on top) at random.
