BIOL3005 Cognition, Week 3 tutorial

Can you hear what you read?

Tools for the phonological loop lab: serial recall of letters that sound alike or different, with and without articulatory suppression (Conrad, 1964; Murray, 1968). Textbook Chapter 3, §3.3 and §3.4.

+
Six letters appear one at a time. Watch, then type them in the order you saw them.

What the task is doing

You see six letters, one per second, and then type them back in order. Half the lists use letters that sound alike (B, C, D, G, P, T, V), half use letters that sound different (F, H, K, Q, R, W, Y).

The letters are only ever shown, never spoken. If similar-sounding lists are still harder, your memory must be converting what you see into sound: the phonological similarity effect.

In half of the task you silently mouth “the, the, the” while the letters appear. That articulatory suppression ties up the inner voice that turns print into sound. The textbook predicts it should make the similarity effect disappear.

Run the task

The task runs full screen and takes about 12 minutes. You need a keyboard.

  1. Six letters appear one at a time in the centre of the screen. Watch them all.
  2. Then type them in the order you saw them, using a dash (-) for any letter you cannot remember, and press Enter.
  3. In one half of the task you will silently mouth “the, the, the” (about twice a second) from the moment the cross appears until you start typing. Move your lips, but make no sound.

Each half starts with two practice lists. When you finish, your results appear on the My results tab.

Settings for tutors

Two halves, normal and suppression, in random order for each student. Each half has 2 practice lists with feedback, then equal numbers of similar and dissimilar lists in random order. Each list: fixation 1200 ms, then six letters for 750 ms each with 250 ms blanks (one letter per second). Letters within a list never repeat. Scoring is strict serial recall: a letter counts only in its correct position.

Your pattern

One person’s numbers are noisy: with eight lists per condition, a difference of ten points can appear or vanish by chance. Your own pattern is interesting; the class pattern is the better evidence.
