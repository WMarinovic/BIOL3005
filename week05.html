BIOL3005 Cognition, Week 5 tutorial

Depth, not effort

Tools for the levels-of-processing lab: case, rhyme and sentence questions about words, followed by a surprise recognition test (Craik & Tulving, 1975). Textbook Chapter 5, §5.3 and §5.5.

A question appears, then a word. Answer the question about the word: J for yes, F for no.

What the task is doing

Before each word you get one of three questions. Case: is it in capital letters? Rhyme: does it rhyme with a given word? Sentence: would it fit a given sentence?

The questions force different depths of processing: the look of the word, its sound, or its meaning. Nobody tells you your memory will be tested.

A surprise recognition test follows. Levels of processing predicts that the deeper the question, the better you will recognise the word, even though you never tried to learn any of them.

Run the task

The task runs full screen and takes about 9 minutes. You need a keyboard; you will use the F and J keys.

  1. A question appears, then a word. Answer the question about that word: J for yes, F for no.
  2. Answer quickly, but read each question properly.

Please do not ask your neighbours what comes next: the task works best if it surprises you.

Settings for tutors

Words are randomly assigned to the three question types for every student, with half of each type requiring a yes and half a no. Each trial: question for 2 seconds, fixation 500 ms, then the word until response (3 s limit). For case questions, yes words appear in capitals and no words in lower case; for the other questions, case is random. A one-minute arithmetic filler separates encoding from the surprise recognition test, which mixes all studied words with an equal number of new words of similar length (J = seen before, F = new).
