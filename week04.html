BIOL3005 Cognition, Week 4 tutorial

Remembering without knowing it

Tools for the implicit-memory lab: repetition priming in lexical decision, followed by a surprise recognition test. Textbook Chapter 4, §4.3 and §4.5.

+
A string of letters appears. Press J if it is an English word and F if it is not, as fast as you can.

What the task is doing

Part 1: you rate how pleasant some words are. Nobody mentions memory.

Part 2: you decide, as fast as you can, whether letter strings are real words. Half of the real words are ones you rated in Part 1. If you are faster for those, your memory is at work even though nothing asks you to remember: that is repetition priming, a form of implicit memory.

Part 3: a surprise test asks which words you rated in Part 1. That is explicit memory. The key question is whether priming survives for words you fail to recognise, the pattern that amnesic patients such as H.M. show.

Run the task

The task runs full screen and takes about 9 minutes. You need a keyboard; you will use the number keys 1 to 4 and the F and J keys.

  1. Sit centred in front of the screen, with your index fingers resting on F and J.
  2. Follow the instructions for each part. Respond quickly but accurately.

Please do not ask your neighbours what comes next: the task works best if it surprises you.

Settings for tutors

Part 1: words shown one at a time for a pleasantness rating (keys 1 to 4). Part 2: 8 practice trials, then lexical decision on the rated words, an equal number of new words and twice as many non-words, in random order (fixation 500 ms, string until response, 2500 ms limit; F = non-word, J = word). Part 3: surprise old/new recognition of all the real words from Part 2 (J = rated in Part 1, F = not). Which words are rated and which are new is randomised for every student, so item differences cancel out across the class.

Your pattern

Priming effects are a few tens of milliseconds, and the number of studied words you failed to recognise may be small, so your own priming estimate for unrecognised words is very noisy. The class average is the better evidence.
