BIOL3005 Cognition, Week 11 tutorial

Same choice, different words

Tools for the decisions and reasoning lab: framing problems (Tversky & Kahneman, 1981), the Linda problem, and the Wason selection task in abstract and thematic versions. Textbook Chapter 11, §11.3 to §11.5 and §11.8.

Try one

Each card has a letter on one side and a number on the other. Rule: if a card has a vowel on one side, it has an even number on the other. Which two cards must you turn over to test the rule?

What the task is doing

Part 1, decisions. Five short choices. Each exists in two versions that describe the same outcomes in different words, for example lives saved versus lives lost. Each student sees only one version of each, chosen at random, so the class can compare the two halves.

Part 2, a judgement. One question about probability.

Part 3, reasoning. Six Wason selection problems. Three are abstract (letters and numbers, colours, textures); three are about everyday rules (drinking age, overtime pay, cycling at night). The logic is identical in all six.

Run the task

The task runs full screen and takes about 10 minutes. You can use the mouse or the keyboard.

  1. For the decisions, choose the option you would genuinely pick. There are no right answers.
  2. For the card problems, select exactly two cards, then submit.
  3. Answer honestly and do not look back at earlier questions; the comparison between students only works if each answer is your own first choice.
Settings for tutors

Part 1: five decision problems (risky-choice framing of the disease problem, the lost ticket versus lost cash, the jacket and calculator, a gamble versus a lottery, and attribute framing of minced beef). For each, every student is randomly assigned one of the two versions, so the frames are compared between students rather than within them. Part 2: the Linda problem (Tversky & Kahneman, 1983). Part 3: six selection-task problems in random order with card positions shuffled; a problem is correct only if exactly the two logically necessary cards are chosen.
