BIOL3005 Cognition, Week 9 tutorial

Led up the garden path

Tools for the language lab: self-paced reading of garden-path and relative-clause sentences, one word at a time. Textbook Chapter 9, §9.3 and §9.4.

+
Words appear one at a time. Press the spacebar for each next word, at your own pace.

What the task is doing

You read sentences one word at a time, pressing the spacebar for each new word. The time you spend on each word is recorded.

Some sentences are garden paths: “While the man hunted the deer ran into the woods.” You probably read “the deer” as what the man hunted, then stalled at “ran”. Other students read the same sentence with a comma after “hunted”, which removes the ambiguity. The slowdown at the disambiguating word is the cost of reanalysis.

Other sentences compare subject relatives (“The senator who spotted the reporter shouted”) with object relatives (“The senator who the reporter spotted shouted”), which the textbook says are harder, partly because of memory load.

Run the task

The task runs full screen and takes about 7 minutes. You need a keyboard: the spacebar, and F and J for questions.

  1. Each sentence appears one word at a time. Press the spacebar to see the next word.
  2. Read at your normal pace, as you would read a book. Do not rush, and do not go back.
  3. After some sentences there is a yes/no question: J for yes, F for no.
Settings for tutors

Word-by-word self-paced reading in the centre of the screen. Each student reads every garden-path item once, half with a disambiguating comma (randomly chosen per student), every relative-clause item once, half as subject relatives and half as object relatives, and 12 fillers; three practice sentences come first. Relative-clause and filler sentences are followed by a yes/no comprehension question. Word times outside 100 to 3000 ms are excluded from the averages.

Your pattern

Each student reads only eight garden paths and eight controls, and word times vary a lot from word to word, so one person’s difference of 50 ms is not reliable. The class pattern is the better evidence.
