Acknowledgement of Country

This book was written at Curtin University’s Bentley campus, on the boodja (Country) of the Whadjuk Noongar people. I acknowledge the Whadjuk Noongar people as the Traditional Custodians of this land and pay my respects to their Elders past and present.

Noongar people have cared for this Country and handed down their knowledge of it, from one generation to the next, for tens of thousands of years. A book about how people perceive, remember, reason and speak is written here in the company of one of the oldest continuous traditions of human knowledge, and I am grateful to study and teach on this land.

Readers studying elsewhere may be on the lands of other First Nations, and I acknowledge their Traditional Custodians too.

About this book

This book was written for students taking BIOL3005 Cognition at Curtin University. Its twelve chapters move from the history and neuroscience of cognitive psychology, through attention, short-term and working memory, long-term memory, everyday memory, conceptual knowledge, visual imagery, language, problem solving, and judgement and decision-making, to the ways drugs and neurological conditions alter the cognitive brain. Each chapter opens with a short orientation and a set of learning objectives, and closes with a summary, self-check questions, its own attribution statement and its own reference list, so the chapters can be read in order or on their own.
