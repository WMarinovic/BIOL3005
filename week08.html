BIOL3005 Cognition, Week 8 tutorial

Turning things over in your mind

Tools for the mental-rotation lab: is a rotated letter normal or mirror-reversed? (after Cooper & Shepard, 1973; Shepard & Metzler, 1971). Textbook Chapter 8, §8.2, §8.5 and §8.6.

A rotated letter appears. Is it the normal letter or its mirror image? J for normal, F for mirror.

What the task is doing

A letter appears tilted at one of six angles, either as it normally looks or mirror-reversed. You decide which, as fast as you can.

No rotation of the screen can turn a mirror letter into a normal one, so to answer you have to compare the letter with how it should look upright. If you do that by mentally rotating it, your decision time should rise with the angle, as though you were turning a real object at a steady rate. That is the result that made mental rotation the centrepiece of the imagery debate.

At the end you rate how vivid your mental images usually are. Does vividness predict how fast you rotate?

Run the task

The task runs full screen and takes about 6 minutes. You need a keyboard; you will use the F and J keys.

  1. A letter (F, G, J or R) appears, tilted. Press J if it is the normal letter and F if it is mirror-reversed.
  2. Be as fast as you can while staying accurate.
  3. At the end, answer two short questions about how you did it.
Settings for tutors

Four letters (F, G, J, R), six orientations (0, 60, 120, 180, 240 and 300 degrees clockwise) and two versions (normal, mirror), fully crossed and repeated; 8 practice trials with feedback first. Each trial: fixation 500 ms, then the letter until response (5 s limit). Analyses use angular departure from upright (0, 60, 120, 180), so 60 and 300 are pooled, as are 120 and 240. Two questions at the end: a 1 to 5 rating of how vivid the student’s mental images usually are, and the strategy they think they used.
