What gets past the filter?

Tools for the load-theory lab, based on Lavie (1995) and Lavie and Cox (1997), with a dilution condition in the spirit of Tsal and Benoni (2010). Textbook Chapter 2, §2.2 Selective attention and §2.3 Load theory.

+
Keep your eyes on the cross, then flash a trial. Find the X or N in the ring and press the matching key. Ignore the letter outside the ring.

What the task is doing

Your job is to find the target, an X or an N, somewhere in the ring of six letters. A seventh letter appears outside the ring, on the left or the right. It is irrelevant: you should ignore it.

Sometimes the outside letter is the same as the target (compatible); sometimes it is the other target letter (incompatible). If you are slower when it clashes, it was processed despite your intention.

The question is when that happens. In the low-load ring the target is the only letter among small circles. In the high-load ring it hides among other letters. In the dilution ring it is among other letters but printed in red, so it pops out.

Run the task

The task runs full screen and takes about 12 minutes. You need a keyboard with X and N keys.

  1. Sit centred in front of the screen, at a comfortable arm's length.
  2. Keep your eyes on the cross. The letters flash for a tenth of a second.
  3. Press X if the ring contains an X, and N if it contains an N. Ignore the letter outside the ring.
  4. Be as fast as you can while making few errors.

There are three blocks, one for each kind of ring, each with a short practice. When you finish, your results appear on the My results tab, ready to share.

Settings for tutors

Each block has 16 practice trials with feedback, then the test trials, balanced for target letter (X or N), distractor compatibility and distractor side, with the target position in the ring random. Each trial: fixation 500 ms, display 100 ms, response window 2000 ms from display onset, then 700 ms before the next trial. Block order is assigned at random from three Latin-square orders.

Your pattern

One person's numbers are noisy. With a few dozen trials per condition, an interference effect of 20 or 30 ms can appear or vanish by chance. Your own pattern is interesting; the class pattern is the better evidence.

Did the load manipulation work?

Search time is the mean response time on correct trials, averaged over compatible and incompatible distractors. If high load is genuinely more demanding, search should be slowest there. Error bars are 95% confidence intervals.

How much did the irrelevant letter intrude?

Each dot is one student's interference effect: incompatible minus compatible response time. Right of zero means a clashing distractor slowed that student down. The black bar is the class average with its 95% confidence interval.
